When doing digital housekeeping on old backup drives, you inevitably encounter audio formats from the digital stone age:

- **Windows Relics: `.wma`**: Hundreds of CD tracks ripped with Windows Media Player during the Windows XP and Windows 7 eras. Double-clicking them on modern macOS triggers an immediate rejection: *"QuickTime Player cannot open this file."*
- **Mobile Voice Backups: `.amr`**: Precious voice memos from family members or old feature phone call recordings exported as AMR files, which modern desktop players ignore.
- **Obscure Apple Containers: `.caf`**: Apple's raw Core Audio Format files generated by specialized audio recording apps or older GarageBand sessions, frequently rejected by non-Apple editing tools.

These files often preserve irreplaceable family memories, oral histories, or vital legal dispute evidence. They should never be abandoned just because modern media players dropped legacy codecs.

With **AConverter's multi-codec local engine**, you can rescue these obsolete formats on your Mac and transcode them into universal MP3 or M4A files in bulk.

### Step 1: Batch Drag Obsolete Files

### Step 2: Choose Optimal Output Presets
* **For Voice Memos & Call Recordings (AMR format)**:
  - AMR was designed for low-bitrate human speech (typically 12.2 kbps). Converting to 320 kbps wastes disk space without improving fidelity.
  - **Recommended Preset**: Format **MP3**, bitrate **64 kbps or 128 kbps**, sample rate **44,100 Hz**. A 1-minute voice recording stays under 1 MB, perfect for email distribution and cloud backup.
* **For Music Tracks & CD Rips (WMA format)**:
  - **Recommended Preset**: Format **MP3** at **320 kbps**, preserving the maximum acoustic dynamic range of the original capture.
