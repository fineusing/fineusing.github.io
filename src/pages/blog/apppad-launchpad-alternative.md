macOS 26 changes how you open and organize apps. For many users, Launchpad was the fastest path from thought to action, and losing it breaks long‑built muscle memory. AppPad exists to bring that flow back.

## Built like a native feature
AppPad is written in Swift and AppKit, so it behaves like a system‑level experience instead of a third‑party overlay. Launching apps is instant, animations stay smooth, and it remains lightweight even on older Macs.

## Your layout, preserved
If you already organized Launchpad, AppPad keeps that structure. Apps show up in the familiar grid, folders stay grouped, and you can continue using the same mental model without rebuilding your workspace.

## Shortcuts that feel effortless
Open AppPad with a hotkey, a trackpad gesture, or a hot corner. The goal is not just “feature complete” but “effortless,” so you never have to think about how to open it.

## A focused tool, not a launcher suite
AppPad does one thing extremely well: bring back a fast, clean app launcher that feels native. That focus is what makes it reliable.

If Launchpad was part of your daily rhythm, AppPad brings it back — without compromises.
