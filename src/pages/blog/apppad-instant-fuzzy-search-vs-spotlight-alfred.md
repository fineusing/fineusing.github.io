In the Mac productivity community, there is an enduring philosophical debate:

**How should you launch applications: via a pure keyboard search bar (Spotlight, Raycast, Alfred), or using a visual icon grid (Launchpad)?**

Keyboard purists insist: *"Type two letters and hit Enter—your fingers never leave home row. Why would anyone click icons?"*  
Visual thinkers counter: *"Who remembers the exact names of 200 apps? When I only use a utility once a month, I need to see its icon and category folder to remember what it does!"*

Both camps make valid points because they tap into two fundamentally different modes of human cognition: **lexical recall** vs. **spatial recognition**.

Here is why pure text and pure visual launchers both have critical blind spots, and how [AppPad](https://fineusing.com/installer/AppPad-Installer.dmg) merges their strengths into a seamless **zero-click type-to-filter experience**.

## 3 Fatal Blind Spots of Pure Text Command Bars

Command bars like Spotlight or Raycast are lightning fast when you know the exact binary name (`code`, `slack`, `term`). But in three everyday scenarios, text-only search falls completely flat:

1. **Tip-of-the-Tongue Syndrome**: You downloaded an audio stem splitter or SVG optimizer two weeks ago. You recall that the icon was teal and the developer was European, but the name wasn't "Audio" or "Splitter". You type three queries into Spotlight with zero matches, give up, and open Finder to scroll manually;
2. **Browsing and Discovery Needs**: Often, you are not seeking a single predetermined tool—you want to survey your options: *"What video transcoding utilities do I currently have installed on this Mac?"* or *"Let me see which mind-mapping tools are available."* A text input box cannot give you this holistic overview;
3. **Typing and Input Method Friction**: If your keyboard is toggled to a secondary language input mode or if you mistype a single character, text bars fail silently, forcing you to backspace and re-type.

## The Sluggishness of Legacy Launchpad Search

Why didn't users simply search inside Apple's legacy Launchpad?

Because Apple's implementation was clunky:
- You had to explicitly click the tiny search box at the top or hit `⌘F` before typing;
- The search engine lacked fuzzy matching—a single missing letter or typo returned zero results;
- The grid filtering animations were rigid and stuttered under heavy libraries.
